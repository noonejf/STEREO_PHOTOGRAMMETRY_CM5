\begin{abstract}
This report presents the SRR-level architecture, Concept of Operations, mission requirements, and system requirements for the LINKU mission. LINKU is defined as a multi-satellite tether experiment composed of SAT-A, SAT-B, and SAT-C. SAT-A acts as the carrier satellite, while SAT-B and SAT-C form the tether-experiment payload and become the two end bodies of a non-conductive tethered system after separation. The report describes the transition from the integrated SAT-ABC configuration to the coupled SAT-BC configuration and finally to the deployed SAT-B/SAT-C tethered configuration.

The report defines the system configuration, SAT-B/SAT-C separation and tether deployment mechanism, tether storage and passive braking concept, measurement architecture, and main subsystem analyses. The measurement approach combines camera observations, GNSS-derived relative motion, tether-tension measurements, and telemetry to support comparison with pre-flight tether-dynamics simulations and ground-test results. The subsystem analyses cover electrical power, communications, attitude determination and control, and structural configuration.

Using the mission objectives, Concept of Operations, system architecture, and subsystem analyses as a basis, the report defines requirements allocated to deployment, measurement, communications, power, ADCS, structural, data-handling, and operational functions. It also presents an SRR-level verification approach based on analysis, testing, inspection, and demonstration to support progression toward PDR and Engineering Model development.
\end{abstract}
