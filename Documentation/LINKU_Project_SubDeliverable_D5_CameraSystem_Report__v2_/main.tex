\begin{abstract}
This report presents the conceptual design, optical characterization, experimental validation, and maturation roadmap of the LINKU stereo vision system, developed to support visual monitoring and three-dimensional reconstruction of a tethered satellite configuration in Low Earth Orbit. The system is intended to complement conventional telemetry by providing direct geometric information about tether deployment, spatial configuration, curvature, and post-deployment dynamic behavior.
\\\\
Orbital tether observation presents a non-conventional computer vision problem. The tether is expected to appear as a thin, low-texture structure observed against a predominantly dark background under highly directional solar illumination. At mission-relevant distances of up to approximately 100 m, a millimeter-scale tether progressively transitions from a geometrically resolvable object to a sub-pixel radiometric feature. Consequently, the reconstruction problem cannot be addressed reliably using standard texture-based photogrammetry alone.
\\\\
To evaluate this problem, the study combines optical observability analysis, controlled laboratory experimentation, and comparative assessment of three reconstruction approaches: conventional photogrammetry, geometry-driven stereo reconstruction, and exploratory AI-assisted processing. The analysis quantified the geometric and radiometric limits of representative COTS camera configurations, while the experimental campaign reproduced key orbital-like visual constraints, including low environmental texture, dark backgrounds, directional illumination, and thin tether-like targets.
\\\\
The results show that conventional photogrammetry is unsuitable for isolated tether reconstruction under low-texture conditions due to its dependence on stable visual features and background texture. In contrast, the proposed geometry-driven framework successfully reconstructed planar and volumetric tether configurations using centerline extraction, geometric continuity, stereo correspondence, and constrained triangulation. AI-based methods were found to be more appropriate as supporting tools for segmentation, topology interpretation, and ambiguity resolution rather than as primary scientific reconstruction engines.
\\\\
Overall, this work demonstrates the feasibility of optical tether reconstruction under representative orbital-like conditions and establishes a physically interpretable reconstruction strategy for future tether monitoring missions. The report also defines the main maturation steps required to evolve the current laboratory prototype toward a flight-capable visual observation system.

\end{abstract}
